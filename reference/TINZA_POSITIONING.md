# WHY TINZA — the positioning page
*Written 27 Jul 2026, the week Checkers launched Dinner Done Better with Jamie Oliver. Read this at 1am when the fear knocks. Use it for launch copy.*

---

## The one-line answer
**A supermarket tells you what to buy. Tinza tells you what it costs, how much you need, and how to cook it properly — for every occasion in a South African life, not just Tuesday's dinner.**

---

## What the giants prove (not what they threaten)
Checkers has now run four Jamie Oliver campaigns and spent serious money on the "what's for dinner" problem. That is the biggest retailer in the country proving, in public, that this pain point is worth money in South Africa specifically. Campaigns are seasonal marketing; they end when the budget does. Tinza is a product; it ends never.

## The retailer's cage (why they can't build Tinza)
A retailer's recipes exist to grow the basket. That incentive forbids the things Tinza is made of:
- **Honest costing.** They will never show a green food-cost that says the bones-and-beans version beats the R36 cut, or that the budget fork should lead. Tinza's two-cost display exists precisely because no retailer can publish it.
- **Retailer neutrality.** Tinza names what you BUY, never where. It works for the Shoprite, Boxer, PnP and spaza-shop majority — the households a premium delivery app doesn't reach. Neutrality is also the negotiating position: a neutral app can partner with anyone.
- **The whole trolley, not one dinner.** A campaign generates one recipe's basket. Tinza's portion brain generates a week's plan, a 40-guest buffet, a braai for ten with bone-aware grams and appetite taper — one guest count, one plan, one shopping list, one cost.
- **Occasions.** Weeknight supper is one shelf of Tinza. Nobody's celebrity campaign does kiddies party themes, buffet maths, finger-food pieces-per-person, Tiny Tummies, or a mood shelf for "I'm not feeling well."
- **SA-first soul.** 1,600+ recipes in the WOW voice — Michelin-chef-to-grandma, why-led, buy-names, leaveners in grams. Durban Mutton Curry with a R26 budget fork. Potjiekos at R33pp. Cape Malay and boerekos koeksisters, both, cross-linked, with the real history. No global campaign will ever write that.

## The honest risks (name them, own them)
- **The shoppable basket is real convenience.** Recipe → ingredients at your door in an hour raises everyone's expectations. Tinza's answer: the shopping list is the product; fulfilment is a partnership waiting to happen — after launch, with traction, not via a website form.
- **Obscurity, not competition, kills solo apps.** The threat was never Jamie Oliver; it's launching quietly. Marketing is a workstream, not an afterthought.

## The partnership posture
The Sixty60 integration stays in the ideas backlog as a *post-launch* conversation. The pitch, when the time comes: "Tinza already builds costed shopping lists for SA households — plug Sixty60 in as fulfilment." Retailers ignore forms and chase traction. Be the obvious small partner with the deep product.
