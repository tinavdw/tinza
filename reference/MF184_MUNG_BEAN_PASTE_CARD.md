### ⛔ SCOPE — THIS CARD IS SAVOURY. THE SWEET ONE IS A SEPARATE CARD.

**Bánh tét's filling has no sugar in it at all** — mung bean, shallots, black pepper, salt.
The sweet paste is a **different product** (bánh chưng ngọt, bánh đậu xanh, mooncakes, chè).

🩸 **They are not a sweetness setting on one card.** They keep differently, they are cooked to a
different consistency, and sugar in the sweet one is doing a **structural** job, not only a flavour
one — it binds water, which is why sweet pastes keep and savoury ones do not. **Two cards.**
⚖️ Same shape as §33.8: two products sharing three words. **Do not bridge them.**

## 🧪 HOW TO GET THE NUMBER — THE LADDER BATCH

⚖️ **Do not look for one number. Cook one pot, split it, take each portion further.**
One variable · four points · same pot · same day. **Anything else is comparing two different batches.**

### ⛔ MEASURE AGAINST *COOKED, DRAINED* WEIGHT — NEVER DRY
Soak time and cook time swing yield by 20–30%, so a dry-weight ratio is not reproducible.
**Weigh the beans after cooking and draining. Every percentage on this card is a percentage of that.**

| | cook down until… | roll a rope — does it stand unsupported? | taste — still moist? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | just mashable | ▢ | ▢ |
| **B** | pulls away from the pan side | ▢ | ▢ |
| **C** | holds a spoon-track ~2 seconds | ▢ | ▢ |
| **D** | stiff, edging toward grainy | ▢ | ▢ |

**▢ WINNER = the LAST rung that still tastes moist.**

### 🩸 THE REAL TEST CANNOT BE DONE IN THE PAN
Wrap and boil **a short test log from each rung**, cool it completely, and **slice it cold.**
▢ Which rungs kept the filling **dead centre**, and which let it **migrate**?

⚖️ **That is the pass/fail.** A paste that looks right in the pan and migrates over an 8-hour boil
has failed, and you only find out at the slice. **This is the fact the card exists to carry.**

## 📚 WHERE TO READ, FOR THE SWEET CARD LATER

**Japanese *anko* is by far the best-documented** — *koshian* (sieved) vs *tsubuan* (chunky), with
ratios recorded properly rather than "sugar to taste". **Read it for the METHOD and for the
sugar-as-preservative principle, then set the sweetness for the Vietnamese card.**

🩸 **The traditions genuinely disagree on sweetness.** Japanese *an* sits high; Vietnamese *đậu
xanh* fillings sit noticeably lower. ⚖️ **There is no correct number to look up — Tina's palate is
the arbiter, and that is exactly why no model authors this.**

⛔ **Do not import a Japanese ratio into a Vietnamese card and present it as traditional.** §33.8 —
no near-miss substitutions.
