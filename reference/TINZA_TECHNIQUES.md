# TINZA — Old-School Techniques (classical & French foundations)

Reusable cook's knowledge to weave into classical and French dishes as they're built
(Boeuf Bourguignon, pot roasts, stews, braises, fish pies, gratins, ragùs). Keep the
Tinza voice: explain the *why*, beginner-proof, sensory. Reference these instead of
re-explaining each time.

---

## 🌿 Bouquet garni
A classic French bundle of herbs that perfumes soups, stocks, stews and braises during a
long cook, then lifts straight out before serving — no loose stems or leaves left behind.

**Traditional (fresh):** 4–5 sprigs parsley, 2 sprigs thyme, 1 bay leaf. Optionally wrap
inside a folded green leek leaf or a celery stalk to hold it together and add flavour.
Tie tightly with food-safe kitchen twine, leaving a long tail hanging out of the pot for
easy removal.

**Pouch (dried/loose):** 1–2 dried bay leaves, ~1 tbsp dried thyme, ~1 tbsp dried
parsley. Bundle in a double layer of cheesecloth or a spice sachet, gather and tie with a
long tail of twine.

**When:** any dish with a long, gentle simmer — stocks, soups (pot-au-feu), stews and
braises (Boeuf Bourguignon, pot roast), poaching liquids for chicken or fish.

**How:** drop it in at the start, with the liquid and aromatics. Leave it for the whole
cook, then pull it out by the string before serving.

*Tinza use:* already used in the Classic French Mince Sauce. Make it the default herb
method for French braises and stews; offer dried-herb pouch as the everyday shortcut.

## 🥛 Mock béchamel (thrift white sauce)
A white sauce doesn't need milk and butter. The science is the same in every version: a
roux (cooked fat + flour) thickens whatever liquid you whisk into it.

- **Classic:** butter + flour roux, whisked into milk. Season with salt + nutmeg.
- **Thrifty:** **margarine** in place of butter — cheaper, behaves identically in a bake.
  Use a *hard cooking/baking margarine at ~80% fat* (e.g. Rama brick); low-fat tub spreads
  are mostly water and won’t make a proper roux.
- **Savoury / meaty bakes:** swap the milk for **well-diluted beef stock**. Gives a
  savoury "mock béchamel" that's cheaper and suits meaty dishes (lasagne, cottage pie,
  moussaka) even better than a milk one.
- **Mornay:** stir grated cheese (+ a pinch of mustard powder) into a finished béchamel.

*Tinza use:* shipped as the Béchamel (White Sauce) base sauce in Sides & Basics, and as
the Budget lasagne layer.

## 🧅 Mirepoix / soffritto (the flavour base)
The sweet foundation under most good sauces, stews and braises: onion + carrot + celery,
finely diced and **sweated slowly** in fat until soft and sweet, *not* browned.

- **French = mirepoix.** **Italian = soffritto** (same trio; soffritto often skips celery
  or leans heavier on onion). Cajun's "holy trinity" swaps carrot for green pepper.
- Slow and gentle is the whole point — rushing it or browning it loses the sweetness.

*Tinza use:* in the Classic French Mince Sauce and the Classic lasagne ragù. Make it the
standard opening step for French/Italian braises.
