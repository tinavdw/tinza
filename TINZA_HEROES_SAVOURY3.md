# ★ CARD 4 — CALDO VERDE (Green Broth)

**Region:** Portugal · **Type:** main / starter (soup) · **Serves:** 4–6

**Story:** Portugal's comfort-in-a-bowl, born in the Minho and now eaten nationwide — a silky potato broth strewn with ribbons of finely shredded greens and coins of smoky chouriço, finished with a thread of good olive oil. The trick is the greens: sliced hair-thin and cooked barely a few minutes so they stay bright and just-firm.

**Ingredients**
- Potatoes — 600 g
- Onion — 1 large
- Garlic — 2 cloves
- Collard greens or kale (couve) — 200 g *(sliced hair-thin)*
- Chouriço (smoked pork sausage) — 100 g
- Olive oil — 60 ml *(+ extra to finish)*
- Chicken stock or water — 1.2 L
- Black pepper — to taste *(taste before salt — the chouriço salts the bowl)*

**Method**
1. Soften the chopped onion and garlic gently in the olive oil — no colour.
2. Add peeled, diced potatoes and the stock/water; simmer 15–20 min until very soft.
3. Blend smooth. Season with pepper; taste before any salt (chouriço comes next).
4. Slice the chouriço into thin coins; warm through in a small pan to render a little smoky fat.
5. Return the soup to a gentle simmer; stir in the hair-thin greens and cook just 3–5 min — keep them bright green and slightly firm, never mushy.
6. Add the chouriço. Ladle out, finish each bowl with a thread of olive oil. Serve with broa.

**Variations** — 🏆 Classic Minho (above) · **Meat-free (honest):** skip chouriço, smoked paprika + extra olive oil for depth · **Heartier (honest):** add a drained tin of white beans before blending.

**Leftovers** *(already frugal → refresh, not reinvent)*
- *(easy)* Reheat gently; it thickens overnight, so loosen with a splash of water or stock.
- *(easy)* Serve the thicker leftover as a green potato purée alongside grilled meat or fish.
- *(creative)* Stir into a bean or lentil pot to enrich and thicken it.

# ★ CARD 5 — FRANCESINHA (Porto's Meat-&-Cheese Sandwich in Spiced Sauce)

**Region:** Portugal · **Type:** main · **Serves:** 4 *(it's a big dish — a generous one each)*

**Story:** Porto's glorious excess — layers of bread, steak, fresh sausage and ham, blanketed in melted cheese and drowned in a spiced tomato-and-beer sauce, usually crowned with a fried egg and a mountain of chips. The name means "little French girl," a 1950s riff on the croque-monsieur turned unmistakably Porto. The **sauce is the soul** — everyone guards their own.

**Ingredients**
- Thick white bread — 8 slices
- Minute steaks (or sliced leftover roast beef) — 300 g
- Fresh pork sausage (linguiça-style or good chipolatas) — 4
- Thick-cut ham — 8 slices
- Chouriço — 8 thin slices *(optional)*
- Melting cheese (mild cheddar / Edam) — 300 g, sliced
- Eggs — 4 *(to top, optional)*
- Chips — to serve
- **Francesinha sauce (molho)** — ~500 ml *(the soul — make ahead; see component link)*

**Method**
1. **Sauce first** (make ahead): soften onion + garlic in butter; add 400 g tomato/passata, 250 ml lager, 250 ml beef stock, a bay leaf, piri-piri to taste and a splash of brandy or port; simmer 20–30 min; blend smooth; thicken lightly; season. *(→ Francesinha Sauce make-your-own.)*
2. Cook the steaks and sausages; halve the sausages lengthways.
3. Build each stack: bread · ham · steak · sausage (+ chouriço) · bread.
4. Blanket the whole stack in overlapping cheese; melt under a hot grill or in a hot oven until molten and just golden.
5. Flood with the hot sauce; top with a fried egg if using.
6. Serve at once with chips and extra sauce on the side.

**Variations** — 🏆 Classic Porto (above) · **Lighter (honest):** one meat + less cheese, sauce still generous · **No-egg (honest):** skip the fried egg.

**Leftovers** *(a fresh-eaten dish — 2–3 honest ideas beat padding)*
- *(easy)* The sauce is the treasure — reheat leftover sauce and pour over chips or pasta.
- *(easy)* Reheat a stack in the oven, not the microwave (keeps the bread from going soggy).
- *(creative)* Chop the leftover meats into the sauce as a hash and top with a fried egg.

**goesWith:** chips → *link house chips, else drop* · pairing: a cold lager.

# ★ CARD 6 — FRANGO PIRI-PIRI (Piri-Piri Chicken)

**Region:** Portugal · **Type:** main · **Serves:** 4

**Story:** Spatchcocked chicken lacquered in fiery piri-piri and grilled till the skin blisters — the dish that travelled the Portuguese-African routes (Mozambique, Angola) and came home as a national obsession, and one every South African will recognise as peri-peri. Made for the braai as much as the oven. The **sauce is everything** — garlic, chilli, lemon, a little smoked paprika.

**Ingredients**
- Whole chicken (~1.4 kg) — 1, spatchcocked
- **Piri-piri sauce** — ~150 ml *(the star — make-your-own → link; or shop peri-peri)*
- Lemon — 1
- Olive oil — 30 ml
- Salt & black pepper — to taste

**Method**
1. Spatchcock the chicken (backbone out, press flat) so it cooks fast and evenly.
2. Loosen the skin; rub inside and out with half the piri-piri sauce, the lemon juice, olive oil, salt and pepper. Marinate 2–24 h. *(Sauce → Piri-Piri Sauce make-your-own; shopping defaults to shop-bought peri-peri.)*
3. Grill over medium braai coals (skin-side management to avoid flare-ups) or roast at 200 °C, ~35–45 min, basting with more sauce, until the juices run clear (74 °C at the thigh).
4. Rest 10 min; cut into pieces; serve with the remaining sauce warmed through, plus chips and a green salad.

**Variations** — 🏆 Classic braai/grill (above) · **Oven (honest):** roast + a final blast under the grill for char · **Extra-hot / mild (honest):** dial the chilli in the sauce.

**Leftovers** *(easy-first)*
- *(easy)* Shred into rolls or wraps with extra piri-piri and salad.
- *(easy)* Toss through a rice bowl or green salad with a squeeze of lemon.
- *(creative)* Piri-piri fried rice, or a quick pasta with the pan juices.
- *(creative · authentic)* Strip the carcass and simmer into a piri-spiced broth for soup.
