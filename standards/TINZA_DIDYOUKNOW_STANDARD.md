# 💡 "DID YOU KNOW" — the fourth voice (locked 27 Jun 2026)
*Paste into `TINZA_RECIPE_DEPTH_STANDARD.md`. This is what makes Tinza an EXPERIENCE, not a database.*

## Why this exists (the Tinza reason-to-be)
> "In an age where everyone is glued to screens, cooking is still real — and we can merge the screen with the real." — Tina

Most recipe apps pull you deeper into the screen. Tinza uses the screen to push you BACK into the world: hands in flour, something simmering, a fact rattling in your head while you stir. **Did You Know** is the field that does this. Even if you never cook the dish you're looking at, you come away wondering about something — or planting basil on your windowsill.

## The field
- **Name:** `didYouKnow` (shown as "💡 Did You Know")
- **Length:** 1–2 sentences. Short enough to read mid-browse, rich enough to stick.
- **Voice:** warm, informed, a little delighted — the Tinza tone. Never dry, never a textbook.
- **On EVERY dish** — heroes and simple dishes alike. No boring corners.
- **Rule:** pick the MOST SURPRISING angle for THAT dish. Never a dutiful fact.

## The four flavours (choose the freshest per dish)
1. 🏛️ **Heritage** — the origin, the story. *(Worcestershire was an abandoned, accidentally-fermented barrel.)*
2. 🔬 **Science** — why a technique works. *(Browning is the Maillard reaction, not "sealing in juices" — that's a myth.)*
3. 🌱 **Grow-your-own** — the nudge into the real. THE TINZA SIGNATURE. *(Basil thrives on a sunny windowsill; spring onions regrow in a glass of water; ~2m² of bed grows a steady supply of lettuce.)*
4. 💡 **Trivia / charm** — the delightful, memorable, useless-but-lovely fact.

## Grow-your-own = the signature strand
Nobody else's recipe app quietly turns cooks into growers. Wherever a fresh herb or easy-grow veg appears (basil, parsley, mint, rosemary, spring onion, lettuce, chilli, tomato, garlic), lean toward a grow-your-own nudge. It encourages self-sufficiency, ties to the real-food / Fermentastic worldview, and is unmistakably Tinza. Keep it light — an invitation, never a lecture.

**Guiding framing: "not everyone lives on a farm, but most at least have a window."** Meet people where they are — no guilt, no smallholding required. A windowsill, a pot, a glass of water is enough to start. The farm is Tina's life; the windowsill is theirs; the thread is the same.

**The founder's true proof (use as a real Did-You-Know):** *"Fourteen years ago I spent about R100 on heirloom garlic seed. I still plant from that same line every season — and my lettuce too. One small once-off, years of food. That's the quiet magic of growing even a little of your own."* — Tina. The maths alone (R100 once vs buying garlic weekly for 14 years) sells the whole idea, and it's TRUE, which is why it lands.

## Worked examples (live, on dishes we've built)
- **Farmhouse Beef Stew** (🔬): *"Browning the meat doesn't 'seal in the juices' — that's a century-old myth. It's the Maillard reaction: sugars and proteins forming hundreds of new flavour compounds. That dark fond on the pot is pure flavour."*
- **Farmhouse Beef Stew** (🏛️, alt): *"Worcestershire sauce began as a failure — two chemists abandoned a foul-tasting barrel in their cellar in 1837, found it months later transformed by fermentation, and bottled it."*
- **Lemon & Herb Roast Chicken & Veg** (🌱): *"That sprig of thyme or rosemary? Both are hardy perennials that thrive in a pot on a sunny sill or step — snip what you need and they keep giving for years."*
- **Roast Chicken, alt** (🔬): *"Resting the bird isn't impatience-testing for nothing — as it cooks, juices rush to the centre; resting lets them redistribute, so they stay in the meat instead of running onto the board."*
- **(any basil dish)** (🌱): *"Basil grows happily on a kitchen windowsill from a single supermarket pot — pinch the tops often and it bushes out instead of bolting."*
- **(any spring-onion dish)** (🌱): *"Don't bin the white roots — stand them in a glass of water on the sill and they regrow within days, almost indefinitely."*
