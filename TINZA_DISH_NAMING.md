## Where naming sits in the pipeline (do NOT name a thin recipe)

Naming is the **LAST** step, derived from a *finished* recipe:

**1. Recipe upgrade** (depth standard — real ingredients + proper method)
**→ 2. Build version categories** (2 / 4 / 5 versions by popularity; obscure = few/none)
**→ 3. Derive the name** from the finished recipe — parent dish + each version

A 6-ingredient skeleton has nothing to name *from*. Upgrade first, then the standout to name reveals itself. Never name upward from a thin recipe — fix the recipe.

---

## Governing principle (read this first)

**The name is DERIVED from the recipe — never invented then checked.**
Read the ingredients + method first, then let the name fall out of what's actually there. This makes "true" automatic.

The name comes from one of two honest sources:

1. **Descriptive** — names an ingredient that's in it, or the method used.
   *Red Wine Beef Stew · Lemon-Herb Roast Chicken · Slow-Braised Lamb · Pan-Seared Steak*
2. **Result** — names the honest outcome that ingredient or method produces.
   *long braise → Falling-off-the-bone · hot roast → Crispy-Skin / Golden · slow-simmered gravy → Velvety*

If nothing in the recipe stands out, it keeps a plain true name. **A plain-but-true name beats a nice-but-false one, every time.**

---

## The two layers — keep them separate

| Layer | Job | Length |
|---|---|---|
| **The NAME** | One enticing hook. Makes you want to chomp it. | Title-short |
| **The BLURB** (below name) | The lush full sentence. Sensory, descriptive. | A line or two |

> Filler words like *Deeply Savory, Satisfying, Aromatic, Root-Vegetable Forward* belong in the **blurb**, never the name. We're naming the dish, not writing the menu paragraph in the title.

---

## The 3 rules (locked)

1. **ONE hook only.** `Red Wine Beef Stew` ✅ — `Hearty Slow-Braised Old-Fashioned Red Wine Beef Stew` ❌
2. **Never lie about an ingredient.** The name is read off the recipe, so this can't happen if you follow the governing principle — no wine in it → no *Red Wine* in the name.
3. **Don't reach for the tired ones.** *Hearty* and *Classic* are on every menu in SA — they've gone invisible. Pull from the bank below instead.

---

## Hook priority (pick the highest one that's TRUE)

1. **Featured ingredient** — strongest, because it's specific *and* honest
   *Red Wine Beef Stew · Brown Butter Mash · Lemon-Herb Roast Chicken · Smoked Paprika ___*
2. **The pull** (make-you-hungry / sensory)
   *Golden · Crispy-Skin · Sticky · Buttery · Caramelised · Falling-off-the-bone · Velvety*
3. **Heritage / nostalgia**
   *Old-Fashioned · Ouma se · Sunday · Farmhouse · **Plaas-style** · Boere-style · Time-honoured*
4. **Method**
   *Slow-Braised · Slow-Simmered · Wine-Braised · Pan-Seared · Oven-Roasted · Flame-Grilled*

## Vocabulary bank — fresh alternatives to Hearty / Classic

**Comfort & warmth** (instead of *Hearty*)
Rich · Robust · Slow-Braised · Slow-Simmered · Deep · Smothered · Melt-apart · Soul-Warming · Stick-to-your-ribs · Wintry · Rustic

**Heritage** (instead of *Classic*)
Old-Fashioned · Ouma se · Sunday · Farmhouse · Plaas-style · Boere-style · Time-honoured · Homestyle

**The pull — make-you-hungry**
Golden · Sticky · Crispy-Skin · Buttery · Falling-off-the-bone · Fork-Tender · Succulent · Caramelised · Velvety · Smoky

> **Plaas-style / Farmhouse** is a Tinza signature angle — fits the real farm kitchen, and nobody else's app is saying it. Use it where it lands.

## Version names (under the selector)

Same spirit, kept **short** because they sit next to the tag emoji.
`🏆 Classic Red Wine · ⚡ Quick Weeknight · ⭐ Ouma's Best · 🌱 Hearty Veg`
(Here "Classic / Hearty" are fine as short version tags — the restraint rule is about the main dish NAME.)

## Worked examples
