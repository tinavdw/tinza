## 0. Build it right the first time
Every recipe is built to full standard from the start — no placeholder cards, no "we'll fix it later." Roughly three-quarters of past time went to going back and fixing; this standard exists to prevent that. **A card is not done until it passes the checklist in §7.**

## 1. Voice — Fable's tone
- **Michelin-to-grandma:** expert precision, zero condescension, total warmth.
- **No shorthand.** Every step names the technique + the temperature/time + a sensory cue + *the reason it matters*.
- **Two audiences at once:** a nervous newbie can follow every step; an advanced cook still learns the *why* and the pro-move. Beginner-proof on the surface, technically credible underneath.
- **Quiet kitchen energy** — calm, warm, human. SA warmth where the dish is South African; adventure for World Kitchen; and so on per section.

## 2. The dish must earn its place
- Every shelf is a **balance**: a few tried-and-tested favourites *plus* genuinely new and unusual ones.
- **Nothing bland.** Even the classics carry a spark — a unique twist, an unusual angle, a properly jacked-up version. If it's googleable exactly as-is, it doesn't ship as-is.
- **Shelf-WOW Law:** remove and replace boring recipes — never disguise a dull recipe with nice words.

## 3. Every card carries all of these
- **How This Feels** — one unique sensory/nostalgic line (never repeated).
- **didYouKnow (the moat)** — a surprising true fact or story that makes the card impossible to find elsewhere. Never reuse an angle.
- **Ingredients** — buy-names matching PRICE_DB; per-person **and** total per line; one per line; prep goes in the method, not the name.
- **Method** — why-led; every make-or-break step named with technique + temp/time + a sensory checkpoint + the reason. No bare "mix" or "bake."
- **Tip** — the one make-or-break insight.
- **Storage / freezes / fridgeDays** — always.
- **goesWith** — real, named library dishes, verified (C4).
- **Versions** — where fame warrants (5/4/2/1 by fame) **or** where one technique carries many fillings (e.g. the swirl rolls).

## 4. Different criteria for different dishes
The core (§1–§3, §5) is always the same. These are the extra make-or-break points each *type* must nail:

- **Breads & doughs** — leavener in grams; liquid temperature; hydration/tackiness; knead cue; two proves with cues; oven temp (+ steam where needed); crumb/crust target.
- **Cakes & bakes** — leavener in grams; creaming/folding technique + why; tin prep; oven temp + doneness (skewer/spring-back); cooling; icing/glaze.
- **Braai & grilled meat** — named SA cut; Portion Brain grams; heat zone; internal temp or doneness cue; rest time.
- **Stews, curries & pots** — build the layers (brown first); real named spices (never a blanket "curry powder"); simmer time; reduction cue; staged salt.
- **Ferments** (your wheelhouse) — culture/starter; time + ambient temperature; salt %; readiness signs; a safety note.
- **Cold / no-bake / fridge** — setting and chill time + temp; assembly order; clean-slice cues.
- **Sauces, dressings & spice blends** — real named spices + ratios; emulsion or bloom technique; storage + shelf life.
- **Salads** — components and prep; dress-when timing; dressing cross-linked to Spice.

## 5. Non-negotiable rules
- **Leavener Law** — baking powder / bicarb / yeast always in **g or ml**, never "to taste."
- **Real named spices**; cured / salty / brined ingredients = **no added salt + say why**.
- **No retailer names.**
- **PRICE_DB buy-names**, costed (per-person + total).
- **C4** — goesWith verified against the real library *before* writing.
- **Beginner-proof _and_ advanced-credible.**
