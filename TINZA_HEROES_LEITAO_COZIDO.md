# ★ CARD 7 — LEITÃO DA BAIRRADA (Bairrada-Style Roast Suckling Pig)

**Region:** Portugal · **Type:** main · **Serves:** 6

**Story:** The pride of the Bairrada, where whole suckling pigs are lacquered with a garlic-and-pepper lard paste (*tempero*) and roasted in wood ovens until the skin turns to glass. It's a festa dish, served in pieces with the crackling, orange, salad and a glass of sparkling Bairrada. The technique — the tempero, the dried skin, the crackling — is the same whether it's a whole pig or a Sunday joint.

**Ingredients** *(the realistic home version — whole-pig note in variations)*
- Pork belly or shoulder, skin on — 2 kg
- Garlic — 1 whole head
- Pork lard (banha) or butter — 60 g
- Black pepper — 2 tsp, coarsely ground
- Coarse salt — 1 tbsp *(added here — pork isn't cured; the rub is the seasoning)*
- Bay leaves — 3
- White wine — 100 ml
- To serve: orange slices, green salad, bread or chips

**Method**
1. **Tempero:** pound the garlic, lard, black pepper, salt and a bay leaf into a paste. *(→ optional make-your-own paste.)*
2. Score the skin finely. Rub the paste hard into the flesh side and into the scoring. Dry the skin uncovered in the fridge overnight — dry skin = crackling.
3. Roast hot (220 °C) 20–30 min to blister the skin, then drop to 170 °C and roast until meltingly tender, basting with the white wine and pan juices.
4. Finish with a final high blast for glass-crisp crackling.
5. Rest; cut into pieces so every piece keeps its crackling; serve with orange, salad and bread.

**Variations** — 🏆 **Authentic whole suckling pig** (Bairrada, spit or large wood oven — same tempero, scaled up, the real showpiece) · **Oven-belly home version** (default above) · **Lighter (honest):** olive oil for the lard.

**Leftovers** *(easy-first)*
- *(easy)* Pull into soft rolls with the crackling and a little salad.
- *(easy)* Scatter pork and crackling over a green or bean salad.
- *(creative)* Pork-and-crackling fried rice, or a hash with a fried egg.
- *(creative · authentic)* Simmer the bones into a stock for caldo or soup.

# ★ CARD 8 — COZIDO À PORTUGUESA (Portuguese Boiled Feast)

**Region:** Portugal · **Type:** main · **Serves:** 6–8

**Story:** Portugal's grand table-in-one-pot — a slow simmer of several meats, a trio of sausages and a garden of vegetables, all built in layers so nothing overcooks, the rich broth ladled off to cook a bowl of *arroz de cozido* alongside. Every region has its own line-up; in the Azores' Furnas it's famously cooked by volcanic heat. It's a feast, and it's engineered to be reborn as tomorrow's soup and *roupa velha*.

**Ingredients**
*Meats* — Beef shin — 500 g · Pork shoulder or ribs — 500 g · Smoked pork (hock / entremeada) — 300 g · Chicken pieces — 4 · *(authentic: pig's ear or trotter — 1)*
*Sausages* — Chouriço — 1 · Morcela (blood sausage) — 1 · Farinheira — 1
*Vegetables* — Potatoes — 6 · Carrots — 4 · Turnips — 2 · Cabbage or couve — 1 small, in wedges · Chickpeas (soaked or tinned) — 400 g *(regional)*
*Rice* — Rice — 300 g *(cooked in the reserved broth)*
*Aromatics* — Onion — 1 · Bay — 2 · Black pepper — to taste *(salt only if needed — smoked meats & sausages carry it)*

**Method** *(layered by cooking time — the heart of the dish)*
1. Cover the **long-cook meats** (beef shin, pork shoulder, smoked pork, pig's ear/trotter) with cold water; add onion and bay; bring to a gentle simmer, skimming the foam, ~1 hour.
2. Add the **chicken** and the **chickpeas** (pre-soaked if dried); simmer 30 min more.
3. Prick the **sausages** and add for the last ~20–25 min — long enough to flavour the broth, not so long they burst.
4. Add **vegetables by time:** potatoes, carrots and turnips for the last ~25 min; the **cabbage/couve wedges** for the last ~10–12 min so they hold their shape.
5. Check everything is tender; taste the broth, season with pepper (salt only if it needs it).
6. **Arroz de cozido:** ladle off some rich broth and cook the rice in it until fluffy — the classic partner. (Reserve extra broth for soup.)
7. **Serve:** slice the meats and sausages, arrange with the vegetables on a big platter, moisten with a little broth, and bring the broth-rice alongside. Share.

**Variations** — 🏆 **Classic** (above; add pig's ear/trotter + chickpeas for the full regional spread) · **Simplified (honest):** beef + chicken + chouriço + the veg, skip the offal · **Azores/Furnas** → story note only (volcanic cook).

**Leftovers** *(cozido is THE reborn dish — traditionally better the second day)*
- *(easy)* Reheat gently in its own broth.
- *(easy)* Chop the leftover meats and veg into the broth for a hearty next-day soup.
- *(creative · authentic)* **Roupa velha** — fry the shredded meats and veg together into the classic Portuguese leftover hash; or fold through broth-rice for next-day arroz de cozido.
- *(creative)* Mince the meats, bind, crumb and fry into **croquetes**.
