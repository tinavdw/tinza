# ★ CARD 9 — PASTÉIS DE NATA (Portuguese Custard Tarts)

**Region:** Portugal · **Type:** dessert/bake · **Makes:** 12

**Story:** Lisbon's most famous mouthful — crisp, blistered spiral-pastry shells cradling a silky egg-yolk custard, scorched dark on top by a ferocious oven. Born at the Jerónimos monastery in Belém, where the originals are still made to a secret recipe. Two things make them: pastry rolled into thin spiral layers, and an oven as hot as it will go.

**Ingredients**
- All-butter puff pastry — 320 g *(1 roll; shop-bought fine — see component link)*
- Milk — 250 ml
- Cream — 60 ml
- Cake flour — 25 g
- Caster sugar — 180 g
- Water — 80 ml
- Egg yolks — 4 large
- Cinnamon stick — 1
- Lemon peel — 1 strip
- Vanilla — ½ tsp
- Ground cinnamon + icing sugar — to dust

**Method**
1. Heat the oven as hot as it goes (250–290 °C); preheat a sturdy 12-hole tin.
2. Roll the pastry into a tight log; slice into 12 discs. Press each into the tin, working the pastry up the sides in a thin spiral layer. Chill.
3. **Syrup:** simmer the sugar, water, cinnamon stick and lemon peel to a light syrup (~100–105 °C); discard the aromatics.
4. **Milk base:** whisk the flour into a little cold milk, then the rest of the warm milk and cream; cook, whisking, until thickened.
5. Pour the hot syrup slowly into the milk base, whisking; cool slightly, then whisk in the yolks and vanilla. Strain.
6. Fill each shell about ¾ full.
7. Bake in the fierce heat 10–14 min until the pastry is crisp and the tops are blistered and scorched in spots.
8. Cool a little; dust with cinnamon and icing sugar. Best warm, the day they're made.

**Variations** — 🏆 Classic Belém-style (above) · **Shortcut (honest):** shop puff + a simpler custard · **Orange-scented (honest riff).**

**Leftovers** *(best fresh — 2–3 honest ideas)*
- *(easy)* Re-crisp day-old tarts in a hot oven 3–4 min — they revive beautifully.
- *(creative)* Crumble over vanilla ice cream or roasted fruit.
- *(creative)* Layer broken tarts into a quick trifle with cream and berries.

**goesWith:** pairing — a strong espresso (*bica*).

# ★ CARD 10 — BOLO REI (King Cake)

**Region:** Portugal · **Type:** dessert/bake · **Serves:** 8–10

**Story:** Portugal's Christmas crown — a soft, port-scented enriched bread ringed with candied fruit and pine nuts, eaten from December through Epiphany. Tradition hides a dried fava bean (and once a little trinket) inside: whoever finds the bean buys next year's cake. The dough is a cousin of brioche and panettone — rich, slow-proved, worth the wait.

**Ingredients**
- Cake/bread flour — 500 g
- Instant yeast — 10 g
- Caster sugar — 90 g
- Soft butter — 90 g
- Eggs — 3 *(+ 1 for egg wash)*
- Warm milk — 120 ml
- Orange + lemon zest — 1 each
- Port or Madeira — 50 ml
- Salt — 5 g
- Mixed candied/crystallised fruit — 200 g
- Raisins/sultanas — 80 g *(soaked in the port)*
- Pine nuts or flaked almonds — 60 g
- To finish: egg wash · extra candied fruit · apricot jam glaze · sugar
- *(Traditional: a dried fava bean + a small trinket, hidden)*

**Method**
1. Soak the raisins in the port. Warm the milk.
2. **Enriched dough:** mix flour, yeast, sugar and salt; add the eggs, warm milk and zest; knead in the soft butter until smooth and elastic. Prove until doubled (1½–2 h).
3. Knock back; fold in the port-soaked raisins and half the candied fruit and nuts.
4. **Shape the crown:** form a ball, push a hole through the centre, and open it into a ring on a lined tray. Hide the fava bean + trinket now, if using.
5. Second prove ~1 h until puffy.
6. Egg-wash; press the remaining candied fruit and nuts over the top; bake at 180 °C for 30–35 min until deep golden (foil over if browning too fast).
7. Brush with warmed apricot jam; dust with sugar. Cool.

**Variations** — 🏆 Classic Bolo Rei (above) · **Bolo Rainha (honest):** no candied peel, extra nuts — for the candied-fruit-averse · **Less-sweet / no-port (honest).**

**Leftovers** *(it dries → traditionally reborn — very authentic)*
- *(easy)* Toast slices and spread with butter.
- *(creative · authentic)* Turn stale slices into **rabanadas** — Portuguese Christmas French toast (soak in milk + egg, fry, cinnamon sugar).
- *(creative)* Bread-and-butter pudding — the candied fruit is already in it.
