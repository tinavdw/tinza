**THE LEAD — one law the sauce lives or dies by:**

- **Ponzu is not soy sauce with lemon in it.** It is a sauce where **the acid does the work salt
  usually does**, which is why a spoonful makes food taste *seasoned* rather than sour. The
  proportions are given to be held in the head, not looked up: **equal citrus and soy, a third of that
  in dashi, a splash of mirin.**
- ⚠️ **THE CITRUS NEVER MEETS HEAT.** Not a simmer, not a warm pan, not five minutes to "marry the
  flavours". The aroma is volatile oil and it leaves well below boiling — heat it and you have
  something sour and salty that smells of nothing, *which is a fair description of most bottled ponzu
  and the whole reason to make it.* Mixed cold; if the food is hot it goes on **at the table**.
- **Yuzu is not buyable, so the blend is stated honestly:** lemon for the body, lime for the top note,
  and a **wide strip of peel with no white pith** for the floral bitter edge neither has. Peeler, not
  grater — the oil is in the coloured layer, the pith is the wrong kind of bitter.
- **Strain the pulp, not just the pips.** Torn membrane keeps releasing a coarse bitterness for days,
  so a ponzu clean on Monday is unpleasant by Thursday. Roll the fruit under the palm first — you are
  rupturing the juice sacs before squeezing rather than through the skin.
- **THEN LEAVE IT 24 HOURS.** Nothing dramatic happens; salt and acid diffuse and the peel oil moves
  into the liquid, and it stops tasting like **three things standing next to each other** — sharp, then
  salty, then sweet — and reads as one. *That is the whole difference between a mixture and a sauce,
  and it costs a day of doing nothing.*
- **Correct by ACID, never by salt.** Flat → more citrus. Sharp → more **dashi, not water** (water
  dilutes flavour along with salt; dashi replaces one with the other). Reaching for more soy is how a
  ponzu becomes salty lemon soy sauce.
- **How to use it**, because most people only meet it beside dumplings: a dip · a **complete dressing
  with no oil and no emulsifying** · a five-second finish off the heat · and a marinade that works in
  minutes — *twenty minutes is a marinade, two hours is ceviche.*
- ⚠️ **Said in the open in the method: dashi carries bonito, so the default is NOT vegetarian even
  though nothing in it looks like fish.** §29.3 again. The fix needs no substitution — the budget
  version is the vegan one and it simply has one fewer ingredient.

**MOAT = YUZU, and none of it is etymology.** The most cold-hardy true citrus there is, which is why it
grows in mountain valleys where lemons die — and the toughness comes with the saying: *peach and
chestnut three years, persimmon eight, and yuzu, the great fool, **eighteen***, so they are almost
always grafted and an old tree is an inheritance rather than a plant. A fruit nobody eats as fruit:
knobbly, thick-skinned, mostly pips, grown for juice and peel, with an aroma whose key compounds sit
near the limit of what instruments can detect and still dominate what the nose reports. Then
**yuzu-yu** — whole fruit floated in hot baths at the winter solstice, done for the monkeys at hot
springs too, which is why the internet is full of macaques in yellow water. And fresh yuzu has spent
decades barred from import into a number of countries on plant-health grounds, so what the rest of the
world got was bottled juice — **which brings it back to the jar in the fridge: everyone outside Japan
is approximating a fruit, and lemon plus lime plus a strip of bitter peel is not a compromise anybody
should apologise for.**
